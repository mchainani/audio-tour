# Research and editorial notes

Prepared for the text review draft on **October 18, 2026**. The links below were consulted on that date. Narration is original synthesis; source prose, song lyrics, and literary passages have not been reproduced. These notes are editorial material and must not be narrated.

Municipal tourism pages provide useful site histories and visitor context. They are not always reliable on legends or contested history. Institutional pages describe their own buildings, designs, and organizations; commercial statements are attributed to the business. Secondary references supplement chronology and cultural context. This is a researched editorial draft, not a scholarly critical edition or a live visitor-information feed.

## 01 — Plaza de Mayo

- **Municipal:** [Plaza de Mayo](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/otros-establecimientos/plaza-de-mayo), [Cabildo](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/otros-establecimientos/cabildo), [Casa Rosada](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/otros-establecimientos/casa-rosada), and [Metropolitan Cathedral](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/otros-establecimientos/metropolitan-cathedral). Site functions, foundation history, architectural changes, political associations, and San Martín's mausoleum.
- **Secondary:** [May Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Revolution), [Plaza de Mayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_de_Mayo), and [Mothers of Plaza de Mayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mothers_of_Plaza_de_Mayo). The 1810 crisis, subsequent independence process, Recova, demonstrations, violence, and human-rights history.

**Editorial decisions:** The May Revolution of 1810 begins a process; formal independence was declared in Tucumán in 1816. The present plaza and Cabildo are altered historic places. The Recova was removed in 1884. Casa Rosada color explanations are presented as circulating stories. The Mothers and Grandmothers are distinct organizations. The dictatorship is dated 1976–1983; the Mothers' first gathering is dated April 30, 1977. Discussion of Peronism includes both social recognition and disputes over political power. Neither a balcony nor the musical *Evita* is treated as a complete account of the movement.

## 02 — Mercado de San Telmo

- **Municipal:** [San Telmo Market](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/otros-establecimientos/san-telmo-market) and [San Telmo neighborhood](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/atractivo/san-telmo). The 1897 market, architect Juan Antonio Buschiazzo, building materials, and neighborhood context.
- **Secondary:** [Yellow fever in Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_fever_in_Buenos_Aires), [Afro-Argentines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Argentines), [Argentine cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_cuisine), and [Mate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mate_(drink)). Migration, the epidemic, African-descended communities, food, and drink customs.

**Editorial decisions:** The indoor market is distinguished from the Sunday antiques fair around Plaza Dorrego. The 1871 epidemic contributed to neighborhood change without being made the sole explanation. Afro-Argentine history is not described through the discredited idea that a community simply disappeared. Empanada styles and mate etiquette vary; the chapter gives common examples rather than claiming one nationwide rule. It names foods without guaranteeing a particular vendor or menu.

## 03 — Estadio Alberto J. Armando: La Bombonera

- **Club:** [La Bombonera](https://www.bocajuniors.com.ar/la-bombonera) and [Boca identity](https://www.bocajuniors.com.ar/identidad). Stadium opening, club symbolism, colors, and neighborhood identity.
- **Secondary:** [La Bombonera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Bombonera) and [Boca Juniors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Juniors). Club foundation, stadium development, Genoese associations, players, and sporting history.

**Editorial decisions:** Boca was founded in 1905; this stadium opened in 1940. Xeneize is explained through the Genoese connection. The Swedish-flag color story and chocolate-box nickname anecdote are explicitly qualified. Capacity is omitted because configuration and approvals change. The chapter works outside the stadium and does not promise a match ticket, museum entry, or access to the pitch. It explains the club as a membership institution beyond a televised men's football team.

## 04 — Caminito

- **Municipal:** [Caminito](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/otros-establecimientos/caminito). Street-museum history and neighborhood setting.
- **Secondary:** [Caminito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caminito), [Benito Quinquela Martín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Quinquela_Mart%C3%ADn), [Caminito, the song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caminito_(song)), and [Tango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tango). Artistic redevelopment, the painter's life, song geography, instruments, dance conventions, and cultural origins.

**Editorial decisions:** The street museum was established in 1959. Today's visitor landscape is a deliberate twentieth-century project with older material and social roots. Leftover ship-paint stories are qualified. The lyric's path is associated with Olta in La Rioja; the Buenos Aires street is not presented as the lyric's literal setting. Tango is shared with Uruguay, with African-descended, European, and local contributions. The joint 2009 UNESCO inscription is supported here through the secondary tango account: UNESCO's direct record could not be read during this research session. No lyrics are reproduced. Milonga conventions are described as variable social practices, and social tango is distinguished from staged spectacle.

## 05 — Puente de la Mujer

- **Municipal:** [Puente de la Mujer](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/otros-establecimientos/puente-de-la-mujer). Completion, tango interpretation, and public setting.
- **Designer:** [Santiago Calatrava project record](https://calatrava.com/projects/puente-de-la-mujer-buenos-aires.html). Structural design, rotating mechanism, and dimensions.
- **Secondary:** [Puerto Madero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Madero). Dock development, later obsolescence, redevelopment, and street naming. The human-rights context also draws on the Mothers reference in chapter 1.

**Editorial decisions:** The bridge was completed in 2001. Approximately 102 meters describes the rotating central span, not the entire crossing. It turns 90 degrees rather than lifting like a bascule bridge. Movement during a visit is not promised. Tango imagery is identified as an interpretation. The 1989 redevelopment institution and the 2001 national crisis place the architecture in its urban and economic setting.

## 06 — Floralis Genérica

- **Municipal:** [Floralis Genérica](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/otros-establecimientos/floralis-gen%C3%A9rica). Eduardo Catalano's gift, inauguration, location, materials, and intended symbolism.
- **Secondary:** [Floralis Genérica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floralis_Gen%C3%A9rica) and [Eduardo Catalano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Catalano). Six petals, mechanical operation, periods of failure, storm damage, and the architect's career.

**Editorial decisions:** Unveiled in 2002. Descriptions differ between roughly 20 and 23 meters in height; narration says “more than twenty meters.” The intended opening-and-closing mechanism is distinguished from current operating condition. December 2023 storm damage is historical context, not a claim about today's restoration stage. No operating timetable or unobstructed access is guaranteed. Interpretation of maintenance and collective work is the guide's commentary.

## 07 — Recoleta Cemetery Experience

- **Municipal:** [Recoleta Cemetery](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/otros-establecimientos/recoleta-cemetery). Opening in 1822, styles, site history, and notable tombs.
- **Secondary:** [La Recoleta Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Recoleta_Cemetery), [Eva Perón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Per%C3%B3n), and [Liliana Crociati de Szaszak's tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Liliana_Crociati_de_Szaszak). Burial chronology, political biography, and memorial details.

**Editorial decisions:** The municipal visitor page repeats the Rufina Cambaceres buried-alive story as fact. This draft treats it as folklore without a secure medical record. The municipal page also compresses Eva's burial story misleadingly. The sequence used here is death in 1952; seizure after the 1955 coup; secret burial in Milan; return to Perón in Spain in 1971; return to Argentina in 1974; Duarte vault burial in 1976. Women's suffrage was legislated in 1947, with first participation in a national election in 1951; earlier campaigners are acknowledged. Architectural symbols are possibilities for interpretation rather than a universal code. The narrative provides no turn-by-turn tomb route and does not promise admission.

## 08 — El Ateneo Grand Splendid

- **Municipal:** [El Ateneo Grand Splendid bookstore](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/otros-establecimientos/el-ateneo-grand-splendid-bookstore) and [Books and literature](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/article/books-literature). The 1919 theater, architects, dome painter, Glücksmann, radio/recording associations, 2000 conversion, and literary culture.
- **Secondary:** [Jorge Luis Borges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Luis_Borges) and [Julio Cortázar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Cort%C3%A1zar). Literary works and reading suggestions.

**Editorial decisions:** A ranking among the world's best or most beautiful bookstores is not a measurement establishing the second-largest bookstore. Theater and recording associations are described at the level of the premises and cultural business; no claim is made that every famous recording took place on the visible stage. Buenos Aires's World Book Capital designation was for 2011. Reading suggestions are editorial recommendations, not a stock list for this branch. The next chapter explicitly acknowledges the return toward Pilar.

## 09 — Basílica de Nuestra Señora del Pilar

- **Municipal:** [Basílica de Nuestra Señora del Pilar](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/otros-establecimientos/bas%C3%ADlica-de-nuestra-se%C3%B1ora-del-pilar). The 1732 opening, architects, patronage, dedication, and retained ornaments.
- **Secondary:** [Basilica of Our Lady of the Pillar, Buenos Aires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady_of_the_Pillar,_Buenos_Aires). Colonial architecture and institutional history. Cemetery context also draws on chapter 7's references.

**Editorial decisions:** The Recollect community was Franciscan; Bianchi/Blanqui and Prímoli were Jesuit architects. Those affiliations should not be conflated. The Zaragoza apparition is identified as religious tradition, not independently verified secular history. Colonial silverwork is connected to wider American networks without assigning unverified provenance to each individual object. The basilica remains a place of worship; listening does not require entry during a service.

## 10 — Cueros Antílope

- **Business:** [Cueros Antílope — About us](https://www.cuerosantilope.com.ar/en/pages/sobre-nosotros). Own-workshop/family-business description, experience claim, and Tacuarí 647 address. These are the company's representations, not an independent factory audit.
- **Secondary:** [Economic history of Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_history_of_Argentina). Cattle, hides, trade, export development, and the relationship between rural production and the port.

**Editorial decisions:** No independently verified “largest leather factory” ranking was established, so that itinerary claim is omitted. Antílope is a brand name, not proof of the animal used in a garment. The chapter is material-history and workmanship commentary, not an endorsement or purchase instruction. General explanations of tanning, garment construction, and care are not certifications of this business's supply chain or environmental performance. Item-specific origin, material, repair, and care should be established with the seller.

## 11 — Rosedal de Palermo

- **Municipal:** [Palermo Rose Garden](https://turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar/en/otros-establecimientos/palermo-rose-garden). The 1914 garden, Thays/Carrasco design history, collection, bridge, patio, and poets' garden.
- **Secondary:** [Parque Tres de Febrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parque_Tres_de_Febrero). Park opening in 1875, Rosas's estate, Caseros, and public-park development.

**Editorial decisions:** The larger park's 1875 opening is distinct from the rose garden's 1914 creation. Benito Carrasco is credited alongside Carlos Thays. Exact plant counts and a guaranteed flowering display are avoided. Spring and autumn references use Southern Hemisphere seasons. Rosas's political legacy is acknowledged as disputed; a park name is not treated as a neutral label. Gardening and ongoing maintenance are part of the interpretation, not claims about a specific work crew on a particular day.

## 12 — Delta del Tigre

- **Municipal:** Viví Tigre's [History](https://vivitigre.gob.ar/historia/), [Tourist circuits](https://vivitigre.gob.ar/circuitos-turisticos/), and [Frequently asked questions](https://vivitigre.gob.ar/preguntas-frecuentes/). Tigre/Las Conchas, transport, scheduled boats, grocery boats, central terminal, and excursion context.
- **National parks:** [Pre-Delta biodiversity](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/interior/ambiente/parquesnacionales/predelta/biodiversidad). Sedimentation, riparian vegetation, and representative regional wildlife. **This park is in Entre Ríos, in another part of the wider delta region; the tour does not visit it.**
- **Secondary:** [Paraná Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paran%C3%A1_Delta) and [Tigre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigre,_Buenos_Aires). Geography, modification, ecology, production, and mainland settlement.
- **Regional journalism:** Sabrina García, [“Rincones del Delta: la Casa Museo Sarmiento”](https://w.zonanorteambiental.com.ar/rincones-del-delta-la-casa-museo-sarmiento/), July 15, 2021. Sarmiento's island residence, writing, cultivation interests, and museum history. This is an older historical account, not confirmation of current opening arrangements.

**Editorial decisions:** The delta is not ranked “fifth largest” or described through an unverified uniqueness superlative. Tigre's animal-name association is with the jaguar, while detailed naming stories are treated as traditions. Regional Indigenous history is included without claiming one ethnic group occupied every modern excursion stop. A retrieved “Chaná” page was a disambiguation page and is not substantive evidence for that history; a specialist review would be useful if expanding this short regional passage. Common regional species are not promised sightings. The terminal pin is on land at Mitre 305 and does not identify an island or boat itinerary. Sarmiento's house is an optional destination. Water-level, land-use, and conservation discussion concerns the wider living landscape.

## 13 — Puerto de Frutos

- **Municipal:** [Puerto de Frutos](https://vivitigre.gob.ar/puerto-de-frutos/) and the [Tigre FAQ](https://vivitigre.gob.ar/preguntas-frecuentes/). The former produce port, present crafts/design market, wickerwork, and visitor context.
- **Secondary:** [Tigre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigre,_Buenos_Aires), with chapter 12's delta references. Fruit/timber transport, island production, and the river economy.

**Editorial decisions:** The historic name is explained without promising a fruit-only market today. Mimbre/willow is connected to basketry and furniture, while no claim is made that all merchandise is locally handmade. Individual stalls may differ from the market's advertised hours. Material observations and questions about provenance are invitations to engage with makers, not guarantees about a product.

## Research limits and reuse

Direct UNESCO tango access encountered a CAPTCHA; the ESMA page returned an access error, and the Ramsar record was unreadable. Those pages are not represented as successfully consulted sources. A guessed Grandmothers history URL and a guessed Sarmiento museum URL also failed; no institutional verification is claimed from them. The INYM homepage was consulted but did not materially support the detailed mate discussion, so it is not cited as if it did.

Source disagreements are resolved explicitly above rather than silently combining inconsistent dates or repeating picturesque claims. Broad cultural explanations and the guide's questions are interpretive synthesis; the bibliography is a chapter-level map, not a citation after every sentence. Expanding politically contested or archaeological passages for publication would benefit from specialist review and more primary scholarship.

Access hours, prices, exchange rates, services, rose counts, bridge movement, Floralis operation, business arrangements, and routes can change. None is a bundled service or included-admission promise in this tour. Map coordinates are approximate orientation pins. The site research did not include on-site inspection or current-access certification. See [NARRATION.md](NARRATION.md) for subsequent recording status and measured durations. The screenshots supplied the chapter sequence; their promotional descriptions were treated as claims to check, not instructions to follow or facts to reproduce.
