## Original voice brief

Use a fluent English-speaking guide with a natural Argentine, preferably Buenos Aires/Rioplatense, accent. The voice should sound warm, observant, knowledgeable, and conversational. Aim for the feeling of a guide sharing a city with a small group, with clear English and comfortably spoken Spanish names. No invented biography or claim of personal eyewitness experience is needed.

Keep the accent consistent and natural. Do not spell English phonetically, force a Spanish reading of every English word, exaggerate consonants, or turn the voice into a comic imitation. “Argentine accent” describes a voice and delivery, not a substitute for intelligibility. A Spanish-only voice reading an English script is not automatically a good fit; audition the actual English performance.

Start around **130 words per minute**, generally within 120–150. Let questions, visual observations, and transitions breathe. Slow slightly for historical sequences and unfamiliar names. Use a quieter, direct register for state violence, bereavement, and cemetery stories; avoid melodramatic music or a horror-story delivery. Food, football, art, and market passages can be more animated without becoming an advertisement.

At 130 wpm the full draft is about **102 minutes**, before substantial pauses. Final durations must be measured from the recordings. The text supplies room for a listener to observe; it does not require a continuous walking pace or synchronized boat route.
